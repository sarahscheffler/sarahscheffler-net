Letters for Sarah Scheffler's Sloan Nomination

This info is about Sarah Scheffler's nomination to the 2027 Sloan Research Fellowship, provided for support letter writers.

Deadline: September 15, 2026
(I do not know if there is a later deadline for letters so I am assuming the 15th for everyone.)

Sloan website: https://sloan.org/fellowships

Link to all my materials required:

  • A letter from a department head or other senior researcher officially nominating the candidate and describing his or her qualifications, initiative, and research (I am handling this out of band)
  • CV (updated August 28, 2026): https://sarahscheffler.net/cv-folder/cv.pdf
  • "Two representative articles by the candidate that highlight … her independent research"
    • "What Adults Will (and Won't) Do to Prove Their Age: Empirical Evidence from a Deceptive Web Experiment". Yanzi Lin, Cheng Zhang, Madelyne Xiao, Lorrie Faith Cranor, Sarah Scheffler. USENIX Security, 2026.
      • This work is an empirical measurement of users' reactions when they encounter different age verification methods with a follow-up survey, motivated by the need to understand the willingness of adult users to go through different forms of age verification given recent age verification legislation in the U.S. and elsewhere.
    • "Public Verification for Private Hash Matching". Sarah Scheffler, Anunay Kulshrestha, Jonathan Mayer. IEEE S&P 2023.
      • In the context of hatch matching systems for detecting child sexual abuse material (CSAM) in encrypted content, we propose cryptographic verification systems that allow public users to verify that the hash matching system is being used only to detect CSAM and is not used to censor other benign content, and describe why this partly, but not entirely, addresses privacy and security shortcomings of such systems.
      • Backup link to open preprint

    • "Selection committees recommend that letters of support be submitted by those who can speak objectively and knowledgeably about the quality and significance of a nominee's work. Letters submitted by co-authors, personal associates, or professional mentors are, in this sense, less useful to selection committees than letters submitted by those with more professional distance. The Foundation leaves it up to the candidates to determine who would best be able to speak objectively and knowledgeably about their work."
